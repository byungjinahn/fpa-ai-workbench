Acquisition target screen

The LLM reads filings, call transcripts, and press releases for each target and scores it 1–5 against a fixed rubric. Corporate development sets the weights, and an analyst can override any score. All ten targets are fictional.

RubricA fixed checklist every target is judged on: financial health, strategic fit, risk, valuation, and management sentiment. Using the same checklist makes targets comparable.
Scores (1–5)The LLM rates each target on each item: 1 is poor, 5 is strong. For risk, 5 means low risk. An analyst can change any score.
WeightsHow much each item counts in the overall score. A deal team focused on fit can weight it higher. Move the sliders and the ranking re-sorts.

Controls that make the output usable

Speed only matters if leadership and audit can trust the numbers. These are the controls behind all three workflows.

Human in the loop

The LLM never publishes. Every draft is approved by a named analyst or manager; in the variance demo, the pack cannot be released until each flagged item is approved.

Math outside the LLM

Variances and forecasts are computed by Power Query and Excel. The LLM explains and proposes assumptions; it does not do the arithmetic that lands in a report.

Data protection

Enterprise-licensed AI with no training on company data, SOC 2 compliant. PII stripped before processing. Nothing sensitive goes to consumer AI tools.

Auditability

AI-assisted content is tagged. Prompt templates are version-controlled. Core models stay in Excel so the audit trail is intact; analyst overrides are recorded.

Accuracy tracking

Monthly review of false positives (immaterial items flagged) and false negatives (material items missed). AI outputs were run in parallel with manual work for three cycles before scaling.

Bias checks

Quarterly comparison of AI and analyst outputs for systematic skew in variance categories, forecast assumptions, and target scores.
